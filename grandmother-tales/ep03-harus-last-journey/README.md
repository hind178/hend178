# حكايات جدتي — My Grandmother's Tales
## Episode 03 · Haru's Last Journey (رحلة هارو الأخيرة)

Final episode of *The Train That Never Returned*. Kyoto 1982, with a flashback to 1962.
Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264 + AAC, 161 s, timed to the supplied narration.

### Rebuilding

```bash
pip install skia-python opencv-python-headless scipy numpy   # Linux also needs libegl1
python3 src/render.py preview check.png 5 30 90 120          # contact sheet at given seconds
python3 src/render.py video build/ 4                          # picture (4 processes)
python3 src/audio.py assets/audio/final_mix.flac assets/audio/music_sfx_bed.flac
ffmpeg -i build/picture.mp4 -i assets/audio/final_mix.flac -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest out.mp4
```

To change a shot, edit its function or its row in `SHOTS` in `src/shots.py`, then re-render (delete the matching `seg_*.mp4` files in the build folder).

### Pipeline and limits

- **Technique.** 2D raster animation drawn with Skia: painted gradients and brush blobs for backgrounds, parametric 2D character puppets with cel shading, rim light and boiling ink lines. Animation runs "on twos" (15 drawings/s) inside a 30 fps file. The look comes from multiplane parallax, a paper-grain pass, bloom and per-scene colour grades. There is no 3D anywhere.
- **Not hand-drawn frame by frame.** No human animator drew these frames. Every pose is generated by code. Acting is simpler than in a studio feature: breathing, blinking, lip movement driven by the narration's loudness, head and arm motion, cloth sway and tears. There are no complex full-body walk cycles or hand-drawn in-betweens.
- **Continuity with Episodes 1–2.** The earlier episodes' character designs were not available in this session. The designs here follow the written brief: Haru's coat, Maryam's braids, cream coat and burgundy scarf, her father's dark green coat, the son's navy coat and beige scarf, and the two cranes with red suns. If Episodes 1–2 used different designs, edit the palettes in `CHARS` in `src/chars.py` to match.
- **The cranes.** The narration describes them as "one Japanese and one Iraqi". Both carry the matching red sun; the second is a slightly warmer paper tone.
